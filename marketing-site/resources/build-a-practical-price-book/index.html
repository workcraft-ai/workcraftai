Build a price book you can actually use

A useful price book is a starting point for repeat work, not a promise that every job costs the same. Organize common items so you can quote faster and still adjust for the conditions in front of you.

WWorkCraft AI Field Guide October 10, 2026

When the same services come up often, it helps to keep your descriptions, units, and usual rates in one place. A small, accurate list is more useful than a large catalog you do not trust.

Start with work you quote repeatedly

Look at recent jobs and note the labor tasks, materials, and service packages that come up again and again. Begin with a short list. Add items as they prove useful instead of trying to catalog every possible repair in advance.

Use specific names and consistent units

Name each item so you can recognize it quickly and customers can understand it when it appears in an estimate. Give each rate a unit such as per hour, per fixture, per sheet, or per square foot. Avoid mixing a per-job amount with a per-unit rate.

Separate labor, materials, and bundled work

Decide whether an item represents labor, a material, equipment, or a bundle. If a package includes both labor and materials, say so in its description. This makes it easier to compare actual costs later and helps prevent accidentally charging twice for the same work.

Review rates when your costs change

Supplier prices, wages, travel time, and disposal costs can change. Set a regular reminder to check your most-used rates. When a supplier quote is time-sensitive, use its expiration date instead of treating it as a permanent price book rate.

Adjust the starting rate for each job

A saved rate is a reference. Access, condition, job size, local requirements, material choices, and risk can all change the price. Review every quantity and rate before sending the estimate, and write down any job-specific assumptions.

A practical price book checklist

  • Each item has a plain name and a clear unit.
  • Labor, material, and bundled rates are easy to distinguish.
  • Rates reflect current costs and your business approach.
  • Every estimate is reviewed for the actual job conditions.

A price book helps with consistency; it does not replace current supplier quotes, local tax guidance, or your own review of a job.
