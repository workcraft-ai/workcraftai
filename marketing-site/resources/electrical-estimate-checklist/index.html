Electrical estimate checklist: replacing an outlet

The visible device is only part of the job. A clear estimate states which outlets are included, what will be checked, and what happens if concealed conditions require more work.

WWorkCraft AI Field Guide October 11, 2026

For an outlet replacement, first confirm the number and location of devices, access to the work area, the required device type, and any known symptoms. An estimate should not promise that changing a device will solve an undiagnosed wiring fault.

Record the scope

Identify each outlet location and the number of devices. Describe safe removal and replacement, suitable materials, testing, and site cleanup. Use wording that matches your licensed work and local requirements.

Separate known work from diagnosis

A sample estimate might list “Replace existing outlet — 2 each” and a separate diagnostic or troubleshooting line if the customer reports intermittent power. Do not bury fault-finding inside a flat replacement price when the cause is unknown.

State assumptions and exclusions

Say whether the estimate assumes a serviceable box and existing wiring. New circuits, panel work, concealed damage, permits, and code-required upgrades may need a separate written scope. Confirm which party will obtain required permits.

Set the approval step

Show quantities, unit prices, total, tax if applicable, and how additional work would be authorized. Let the customer approve the stated scope before scheduling.

Before you share

  • Confirm device count, locations, and symptoms.
  • Check licensing, permit, and local code requirements.
  • Keep unknown wiring or panel work out of the fixed scope.

These are scope examples, not local prices or code advice. Verify measurements, permit and licensing requirements, materials, labor, taxes, and your customer’s approval before work begins.
