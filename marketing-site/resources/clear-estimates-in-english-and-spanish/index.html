Make estimates clear in English or Spanish

A translated estimate should preserve the same scope, price, and conditions in both languages. Clear wording and a quick review help prevent misunderstandings.

WWorkCraft AI Field GuideOctober 10, 2026

Language choice is part of customer service. A clear bilingual estimate can reduce repeated explanations and help both sides confirm the same work.

Ask which language the customer prefers

Ask the customer which language they would like to use for the estimate and follow-up. Do not assume a preference from a name, accent, or location. Record the preference so the next message uses the right language too.

Keep the two versions aligned

Use the same quantities, units, options, totals, exclusions, payment terms, and validity period in each language. Translate the meaning of the scope rather than word-for-word phrases that may sound unnatural in a trade context.

Check regional trade terms

A word for a material or task may vary by region. Use plain descriptions and, when useful, include the familiar product name or a measurement. Ask a bilingual trade professional to review terms that could change what work is performed.

Make assumptions and changes explicit

State what is included, what is not included, and which measurements still need confirmation. If a customer asks for a change, repeat the updated scope and amount in the customer’s preferred language before seeking approval.

Review numbers separately from translation

Check that quantities, decimal separators, currency symbols, tax, dates, and totals stayed the same. Keep one authoritative estimate record so the language version does not accidentally become a different price or scope.

Before sending a bilingual estimate

  • The customer chose the language they prefer.
  • Scope and trade terms are natural and accurate.
  • Amounts, quantities, dates, and terms match.
  • The customer knows how to ask a question or approve.

For contracts, safety instructions, permits, or regulated work, use a qualified human reviewer when an inaccurate translation could change rights, obligations, or safety.
