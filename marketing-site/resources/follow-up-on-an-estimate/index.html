When and how to follow up on an estimate

Following up can answer a question or clarify timing. A short, helpful note respects the customer’s decision and keeps communication easy to manage.

WWorkCraft AI Field GuideOctober 10, 2026

The goal of a follow-up is to make the next step easier, not to pressure the customer into a decision.

Set the expectation when you send it

Tell the customer when you expect to follow up and how long the estimate is valid, if that matters for materials or availability. Ask what channel and time of day work best. Avoid promising a price hold you cannot honor.

Use a simple, low-pressure cadence

For many small jobs, one check-in after a few business days is reasonable. If the customer is still deciding, ask whether they need clarification and whether they would like another follow-up later. Stop when they decline or ask not to be contacted.

Make the message specific and useful

Mention the job and estimate reference, ask one clear question, and offer to explain an assumption, option, or schedule. Avoid guilt, artificial deadlines, or repeated messages across several channels.

A simple message you can adapt

“Hi [Customer], I’m checking in on the estimate for [job]. Is there anything you’d like me to clarify? If you’re still deciding, that’s fine—let me know if you’d like me to check back on [date]. Thanks, [Name].”

Keep a brief record

Note when you contacted the customer, what they asked, and the next agreed step. This helps you avoid duplicate outreach and gives you context if the customer replies weeks later.

Before you send a follow-up

  • The message references the right job.
  • It asks one useful question and offers help.
  • The timing respects what the customer requested.
  • You will stop if the customer declines or opts out.

Follow applicable rules for email, calls, and text messages. Honor opt-out requests and your customer’s stated communication preferences.
